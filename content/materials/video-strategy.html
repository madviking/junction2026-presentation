The letter in the drawer

Why resident-first: the reasoning behind 0:40

This is our reasoning, not a sourced statistic. Say it as a choice we made. The facts in it (registers, the gap at the door, the ladder) are sourced in the master narrative.

If we had started with the county
  1. FirstProcurement, a pilot agreement, the data controller role and an impact assessment.
  2. ThenIntegration with the patient record and booking systems.
  3. SeesPeople who already came in. Registers do not record the steps before the first contact, which is where the gap opens.
  4. ReachesStaff and processes, not Hodan's drawer. Equality is not guaranteed by better operations.

Value lands late, and it can miss the person the challenge is about.

What we built: start with the resident
  1. TodayWorks with no contract, no login and no personal data. Any letter can be photographed and explained.
  2. SeesThe step before the door: what she fears, in her language, counted anonymously.
  3. ReachesThe person holding the letter, exactly where the 20 points disappear.
  4. ThenEach adopter (nurse, health centre, county) adds a rung, and each rung has its own measurable gain.

Value from day one, and the county path is still open, step by step.

What the video does not say

  • That language causes the gap. Say "people with another native language attend less".
  • That county-first solutions are wrong. Say why we chose to start with the resident.
  • Anything about Hodan's faith as data. Registries record language and origin, not religion.
  • That Finland proved in-visit kits work. The 56% is from London.
  • Lives saved or net savings. Only cost per extra screen.
