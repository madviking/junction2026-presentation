# Pitch video — five storylines

Five ways to tell Silta in under two minutes. Each one covers all five AstraZeneca criteria,
shows the product working, and gives the Gemini and ElevenLabs moments screen time. They differ in
**what carries the story**: a person, a helper, the demo, the data or the county.

**Deadline:** submission Sun 15 Nov 09:30; judging 14:00. It gets dark around 15:30, so plan outdoor
shots for Sunday morning or treat them as night shots.

## What every version must contain

| Criterion (weight) | The minimum on screen |
| --- | --- |
| Actionability (30%) | Three concrete things a county can do, in one caption block. |
| Insight (25%) | Where the gap opens: **at the first step, getting through the door**. Breast and cervical follow-up show no gap. |
| Data quality (20%) | A source line under every number + an end card: sources, "what we don't claim", link to `/dashboard`. |
| Execution (15%) | Real screen recordings of the working app, not mock-ups. |
| Clarity (10%) | One key message, at most three headline numbers. |

**Key message (shared by all versions):** *Same invitation, about 20 points fewer attend, and the gap
opens at the first step. Silta works on that first step.*

**Number bank** (pick at most three per video; confirm each with Mea before export):

| Number | Source |
| --- | --- |
| 646,392 people, 11.4%, ≈ 1 in 9, have another native language (2025) | Statistics Finland 11rm |
| Breast screening 60.8% vs 83.2% (2024); ≈ −20 to −22 pp in all three programmes | Finnish Cancer Registry |
| Cervical gap 15.6 → 19.9 pp, 2020 → 2024 | Finnish Cancer Registry |
| Africa-born women: 45.1% breast attendance vs 83.1% Finland-born | Finnish Cancer Registry |
| ≈ 16,560 missed screenings a year | Mea's analysis (registry) |
| Colonoscopy after a positive test: −8.8 pp; Helsinki waits 3.4 vs 1.9 months | Registry, pooled 2022–24 |
| Translated letters alone: 46.5% vs 47.4% | Norway trial |
| Self-sampling kit offered at a GP visit: 56% vs ~13% by post | London study |

**County actions (shared caption block):**
1. Put a Silta link where people already land: the invitation letter, the health-station web page, community groups.
2. At the visit: the Finnish one-screen summary, preferences known in advance, and a prompt to offer a self-sampling kit.
3. Measure progress on sub-goal 1.3 with the registry's native-language table the county already gets.

**Before filming, check with Mea which of these work in the demo build:** "help someone else"
mode, preference fields (interpreter language, comfort with a cervical sample, clinician gender),
the self-sampling prompt, code-not-name and delete, the county view.

## 1. "How do I say this?" — one person, a sensitive concern

**Logline.** Leila has had bleeding between periods for weeks. She has no medical Finnish, and in her
culture this is not something you say out loud. Silta lets her tap what she can't say.

**Best for:** emotion, Insight, safety. **Effort:** medium. **Cast:** 1 actor + 1 "nurse".

### Narrative

| Time | Beat | Picture | Sound and captions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:12 | Hook | Night. Phone light on her face behind a half-closed door. She types in Arabic, deletes, types again. | Room tone. Caption: *"How do I even say this?"* |
| 0:12–0:30 | The gap | The bus home, earphones in. Captions over her. | VO: *1 in 9 people in Finland have another native language. They attend cancer screening about 20 points less, and for cervical screening the gap has grown every year since 2020.* |
| 0:30–0:40 | Where it breaks | Pathway graphic: invitation → attend → result → follow-up, with only the first step lit red. | VO: *Once people are inside, follow-up is equal. The gap is at the door.* |
| 0:40–1:20 | Silta | Screen recordings on her phone, one per barrier. | Captions: **No words** → cards in Arabic, audio in earphones. **Not sure** → she types "bleeding that isn't my period"; Gemini matches a card and asks back. **No name** → a random code, delete anytime. **What will they do?** → a "what to expect" card, her preferences, the right door, a Finnish call script. |
| 1:20–1:35 | The visit | Neutral exam room. The nurse reads the summary on a tablet and offers a home kit. Leila's shoulders drop. | Caption: *Offered at the visit, 56% took a self-sampling kit, against ~13% by post.* |
| 1:35–1:50 | The county | Clean caption cards. | The three county actions. |
| 1:50–2:00 | Close | Leila walking out in daylight, then the logo and team. | *Silta: a bridge to the right door.* Sources on screen. |

### What to film and where

- Night doorway and phone-lit face: any apartment, a dark room, the phone as the only light.
- Bus or tram window seat with earphones (HSL; quick, no faces of other passengers).
- Health-station exterior (Kalasatama or Itäkeskus), walking in and out. Exterior only.
- "Exam room": a meeting room at the venue, framed on faces and the tablet.
- Screen recordings in Arabic: cards, the Gemini match, the code screen, the right door, the "what to expect" card, the clinician summary.

### Prepare

- An Arabic- (or Somali-) speaking actor comfortable with the topic, who writes her own lines. Written consent; can stay partly anonymous.
- Earphones, a tablet for the nurse, a plain cardigan (no uniform, no real logos).
- The pathway animation (one graphic, reused in versions 2–4).

### Risks

- The topic needs care: no bodies, no procedures; the screen carries the delicate words.
- Don't claim a female-clinician preference unless it exists in the app and in practice.

## 2. "Can you call for me?" — an adult child helping a parent

**Logline.** Amina, 26, finds out her mother has noticed a lump. She wants to help, but she doesn't
know the Finnish medical system either, and her mother doesn't want to say "breast" to her daughter.
Silta lets them work through it together, then hands over to a professional interpreter.

**Best for:** relatability for the whole hackathon audience, warmth, the interpreter-rights point.
**Effort:** medium–high. **Cast:** 2 actors (ideally a real parent–child pair) + 1 "nurse".

### Narrative

| Time | Beat | Picture | Sound and captions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:12 | Hook | Kitchen, evening, tea. The mother says something in Somali, almost in passing. The daughter freezes. | No subtitles for 3 s, then: *"I found something. I didn't want to bother anyone."* |
| 0:12–0:30 | The gap | The daughter googling, Finnish-only pages, a bank-ID prompt. | VO: *1 in 9 people in Finland have another native language. They attend screening about 20 points less, in every programme. The digital front doors are in Finnish and Swedish, and most need bank ID.* |
| 0:30–0:40 | Where it breaks | Pathway graphic, first step lit. | Daughter: *"Okay. Who do we even call?"* |
| 0:40–1:20 | Silta, together | One phone between them; Somali for the mother, Finnish for the daughter. | The mother taps cards and hears Somali audio (she doesn't have to say the word). The daughter types "she found a hard lump a few weeks ago"; Gemini matches and asks back. The right door; the daughter reads the Finnish call script into the phone. Caption: *Your mother has the right to a free professional interpreter.* |
| 1:20–1:35 | The visit | The nurse reads the summary; the interpreter is booked; the daughter waits outside. "What happens next" card. | Caption: *The daughter books it. She doesn't have to interpret it.* |
| 1:35–1:50 | The county | Caption cards. | The three county actions + one impact line (Mea's slider default). |
| 1:50–2:00 | Epilogue and close | Months later, a screening letter arrives; the mother opens Silta herself, in Somali. Logo, team. | *Silta: a bridge to the right door.* |

### What to film and where

- Kitchen table, evening (60% of the video, one location).
- The daughter on the phone at a window or in a stairwell; the health station's side is just a teammate's voice.
- Health-station exterior: both walking in.
- A mail slot and the letter for the epilogue.
- Screen recordings in Somali and Finnish, side by side if the app supports it.

### Prepare

- A Somali- or Arabic-speaking parent and child (friends, Junction participants). If not available: film the daughter's face and the mother's hands and voice.
- A generic printed screening letter as a prop: no real county logo or letterhead.
- Check whether "help someone else" mode exists; if not, the daughter simply holds the phone.

### Risks

- Two actors with good chemistry is the hardest casting.
- Somali answers by cards (weak speech recognition), so the mother taps rather than speaks. That's true to the product; keep it so.

## 3. "One take" — a live demo as the story

**Logline.** No actors. Timo holds a real phone and walks one person's journey through Silta in a
single continuous take, from "I noticed something" to the clinician's summary and the county view,
with the data framing at the start and the county actions at the end.

**Best for:** Execution, and the three tech prizes (Gemini, ElevenLabs, Aiven). The most credible and
the least risky to make. **Effort:** low–medium. **Cast:** the team.

### Narrative

| Time | Beat | Picture | Sound and captions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:15 | Problem to camera | Timo at the venue, medium shot. | *"1 in 9 people in Finland have another native language. They attend cancer screening about 20 points less, and the gap opens at the first step: getting through the door. We built for that step."* Source line on screen. |
| 0:15–0:20 | Set-up | Caption: *Meet a resident. She speaks Arabic and has a symptom she's not sure about.* | |
| 0:20–1:15 | The demo | Screen recording, full frame, with a small picture-in-picture of hands on the phone. | Pick Arabic → a card plays aloud (ElevenLabs) → free text typed → Gemini picks a card with probabilities and asks back → the right door for Helsinki → the Finnish call script → the visit card → the clinician's one-screen summary in Finnish. Captions name each step, 3–4 words each. |
| 1:15–1:30 | Under the hood | Simple architecture card: Gemini (closed-set match), ElevenLabs (audio in 4 languages, cached), Aiven PostgreSQL + pgvector (content, audio, anonymous visits, county figures). | VO, one sentence per partner. |
| 1:30–1:48 | The county | The county view: anonymous counts by stage and language. Then the three actions. | *"What a county does on Monday: …"* |
| 1:48–2:00 | Honesty and close | End card: not a diagnosis, no identity, sources, `/dashboard`. Team. | *Silta: a bridge to the right door.* |

### What to film and where

- Timo to camera, twice (open and close), at the venue with a calm background.
- A clean screen recording of the whole flow in one take, at full resolution (record two or three takes and pick the best).
- An overhead shot of hands on the phone for picture-in-picture, synced to the recording.

### Prepare

- A stable demo build with seed content in Arabic and Somali; airplane-proof (cached audio, no slow calls).
- The architecture card (one slide-style graphic, palette from the site).
- A script for Timo's 30 seconds on camera, rehearsed until it's natural.

### Risks

- Less emotional; the hackathon audience may find it "just a demo". Mitigate with a strong first line and fast, tight cuts.
- One take is fragile: record clean, then cut it tighter in editing while keeping the order.

## 4. "Follow the 20 points" — data as the story

**Logline.** We make the gap physical. 100 envelopes, or two rows of chairs, travel down a pathway
taped on the floor. At each step we show who drops off, and the audience sees with their own eyes
that the gap opens at the very first step. Then Silta goes exactly there.

**Best for:** Insight and Data quality (45% together), Clarity for non-technical judges, and a memorable
visual for the audience. **Effort:** medium (a set build at the venue). **Cast:** the team + 6–10 volunteers.

### Narrative

| Time | Beat | Picture | Sound and captions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:10 | Hook | Top-down: two piles of 10 invitation envelopes. One labelled *Finnish, Swedish, Sámi*, the other *other native languages*. | VO: *Same invitation. Same programme.* |
| 0:10–0:35 | Step 1: attendance | Hands move envelopes across a tape line labelled *attended*: 8 cross on one side, 6 on the other. Or volunteers in two rows of chairs, 8 vs 6. | VO: *About 20 points fewer attend, in all three programmes. 1 in 9 people in Finland are in the second pile, and the gap is growing.* Source line. |
| 0:35–0:50 | Steps 2–3: inside | The envelopes that crossed move on to *result* and *follow-up*. Both sides move equally. | VO: *Once inside, breast and cervical follow-up are equal. The gap is at the door.* (Optional: colorectal is the exception, −8.8 pp, with Helsinki waiting 3.4 months.) |
| 0:50–1:00 | Who and where | Quick data cards (animated from Mea's CSVs): Africa-born women 45.1% vs 83.1%; half live in three capital-region counties. | Caption: *Not just education: the gap remains after controlling for it.* (Exact wording from Mea.) |
| 1:00–1:35 | Silta at the door | The 4 envelopes that didn't cross. A phone lands on one, Silta opens in Somali; quick montage of the flow (cards, Gemini match, right door, visit card, clinician summary). One envelope slides across the line. | VO: *Silta works on that first step: in your language, to the right door, understood when you get there.* |
| 1:35–1:50 | The county | Caption cards + the impact line: *close a quarter of the gap ≈ X more screenings a year.* | Three county actions. |
| 1:50–2:00 | Data card and close | "What we don't claim" in three lines + sources + `/dashboard`. Logo, team. | |

### What to film and where

- A table top or floor at the venue, filmed top-down (a phone on a tripod or a friend on a chair).
- Painter's tape lines labelled *invited → attended → result → follow-up*.
- Optional chair version: two rows of 10 chairs with volunteers, filmed from the side.
- A short screen-recording montage of the app.

### Prepare

- 20+ envelopes, tape, labels in a clean font (the site's), a tripod.
- Animated data cards from Mea's CSVs (Plotly export or simple motion graphics), each with a source line.
- Mea checks every number and caveat wording.

### Risks

- Envelopes are a proportional metaphor (10 = 100%): say "out of 10" or show the real percentages next to them.
- Less product time (≈ 35 s). Keep the demo montage crisp, with captions.

## 5. "Monday morning at the county" — the decision-maker's story

**Logline.** A screening coordinator at a wellbeing services county opens the registry's
native-language table and sees the gap in her own county. In 90 seconds she sees why it happens,
what Silta does about it, and exactly what she can switch on, and we see a resident's journey
through her eyes.

**Best for:** Actionability (30%) and Clarity for the AstraZeneca judges, who want a follow-up project
with a county. **Effort:** low–medium. **Cast:** 1 "coordinator" + 1 resident (hands only is fine).

### Narrative

| Time | Beat | Picture | Sound and captions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:12 | Hook | An office desk, coffee, a laptop. The coordinator opens the registry table. Zoom on one row. | Caption: *Your county. 2024. Breast screening: 83% vs 61%.* |
| 0:12–0:35 | Insight | She scrolls the pathway: the gap is at attendance, not at follow-up. Animated data cards. | VO: *Same invitation, about 20 points fewer attend, and the gap opens at the first step. Translated letters alone don't change it (46.5% vs 47.4%).* |
| 0:35–1:15 | What the resident sees | Cut to a resident's hands with a phone (Somali or Arabic). Silta flow: cards and audio, Gemini match, the right door in *her* county, the call script, the visit card. Cut back to the clinician summary on a health-station screen. | VO: *Silta follows one person from "I noticed something" to the right door, in her language.* |
| 1:15–1:40 | What the county switches on | The coordinator in the county view: anonymous counts by stage and language. Three actions appear as a checklist she ticks. | The three county actions + *measured with the indicator you already get.* |
| 1:40–1:52 | Impact and honesty | The impact slider at 25%: X more screenings, Y cancers found earlier. Then three "what we don't claim" lines. | Source line. |
| 1:52–2:00 | Close | Logo, team. | *Silta: start with one county. We'd like it to be yours.* |

### What to film and where

- An "office" at the venue or at home: desk, laptop, a mug. The coordinator from behind or in profile.
- Screen recordings: registry table (or our dashboard), the county view, the Silta resident flow, the clinician summary.
- Resident's hands with the phone on a sofa or bus (insert shots).

### Prepare

- The dashboard pages in their final form (they double as the data-quality proof).
- The county view seeded with plausible demo data, labelled as demo data on screen.
- A coordinator actor who reads well in profile; the line is the only spoken one besides the VO.

### Risks

- Least emotional; can feel like a sales video. The resident insert (0:35–1:15) carries the human side.
- Don't show the real registry site as if it were ours; show our dashboard with its sources.

**Combinations that fit 2 minutes:**

- **1 + 4:** Leila's story, with the envelope shot as the data beat (0:12–0:40). Strong emotion and strong insight.
- **3 + 5:** Timo's demo framed by the county coordinator. The safest for the AstraZeneca judges and the tech prizes.
- **2 + the letter epilogue:** the warmest for the audience; needs the right cast tonight.

## Shared preparation (all versions)

- **Screen recordings** in Arabic, Somali and Finnish at full resolution; composited onto the phone in editing, never filmed off a screen.
- **Graphics:** the pathway animation, three data cards, the county-actions card, the end card (sources, what we don't claim, `/dashboard`). Site palette, readable at phone size.
- **Voice-over:** one calm voice (Timo or Milla), recorded in a quiet room; ≈ 250–280 words for 2 minutes.
- **Captions:** burned in, English; any Somali or Arabic on screen checked by a native speaker.
- **Music:** a quiet instrumental bed, generated (Lyria) or royalty-free, low under the voice.
- **Consent:** written OK from everyone on camera; scenes are presented as illustrative, never as real patients.

## Generative AI: where it helps and where it doesn't

Use Gemini for: storyboard frames before filming (never in the video), drafting dialogue that the cast
rewrites in their own words, English subtitles, generic prop text for the letter, the code for data
animations from Mea's CSVs, and at most one or two Veo establishing shots with no people in them.

Don't generate: faces of patients or nurses, clinical interiors, a synthetic speaker presented as a
person, or anything that looks like real footage of a real place or organisation. The ElevenLabs audio
heard inside the app is fine: that is the product working.
