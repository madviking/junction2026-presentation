# Pitch video — "Three doors"

**Idea.** Three people, three cancer screening programmes, the same problem: *where do I go, how do I
say it, and what will happen to me?* Short clips open the video, the data explains why this is a
pattern and not bad luck, and the demo walks each of them through their door.

Each person maps to one programme, so the data and the demo line up:

| Person | Programme | Their barrier | What Silta does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Yusuf, ~55**, a pharmacy, stomach and bowel trouble | Colorectal | Thinks it's nothing, doesn't know whom to ask, symptom vocabulary | QR at the pharmacy → describe in his language → the right door + a Finnish call script |
| **Leila, ~40**, a cervical screening letter | Cervical | The time doesn't fit; fear of undressing, of the unknown, of a male examiner | QR on the letter → what to expect, how to change the time, preferences, a home self-sampling kit |
| **Amina, 26, and her mother Hodan** | Breast | The mother won't say "breast" to her daughter; neither knows the system | Cards in Somali for the mother, call script for the daughter, a professional interpreter at the visit |

**Key message:** *Same invitation, about 20 points fewer attend, and the gap opens at the first step.
Silta works on that first step.*

Suggested languages (adjust to the cast): Yusuf Arabic, Leila English or Arabic, Hodan Somali. Silta
supports English, Finnish, Arabic and Somali; Somali answers by cards, because speech recognition is
weak in Somali.

## Timeline and script (2:00)

### 0:00–0:24 · Cold open: three moments of hesitation

No voice-over. Room sound, a soft music bed. Each clip is about 7 seconds and ends on a pause. A Silta
QR is visible in each, but nobody reacts to it yet (it pays off in the demo).

| Time | Clip | Picture | Caption |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:08 | **Yusuf** | Pharmacy aisle. He picks up a stomach medicine box, reads the Finnish label, puts it back, picks up another. At the counter, a small Silta QR stand. He photographs it, almost shyly. | *Yusuf, 55. "It's probably nothing."* |
| 0:08–0:16 | **Leila** | A letter through the mail slot. Kitchen: she opens it, a Finnish screening invitation with a set time. She looks at the calendar (that day is impossible), then at the letter again, uneasy. A QR in the letter's corner. | *Leila, 40. "What will they do? Who will be there?"* |
| 0:16–0:24 | **Amina and Hodan** | Evening tea. Hodan says something in Somali, almost in passing, and looks away. Amina freezes. | Subtitle: *"I found something. I didn't want to bother anyone."* |

### 0:24–0:28 · Bridge

The three clips freeze side by side as a triptych.

> **Caption:** *Three people. Three screening programmes. One question: where do I go, and what happens there?*

### 0:28–0:55 · The data and the reasoning

Voice-over (≈ 75 words) over animated data cards in the site palette, each with a source line.

> **VO:** One in nine people in Finland has a native language other than Finnish, Swedish or Sámi.
> They attend cancer screening about twenty points less, in all three programmes, and for cervical
> screening the gap has grown every year since 2020.
> And the gap opens at the first step. Once people are inside, follow-up is equal.
> Translating the letter doesn't fix it. What works is preparing the person, and the visit.

| Time | Card | Source line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:28–0:35 | **1 in 9**: 646,392 people (11.4%) | Statistics Finland, 2025 |
| 0:35–0:42 | **Three bars**: breast, cervical and colorectal attendance, the two groups side by side; *≈ −20 pp in every programme* | Finnish Cancer Registry, 2024 |
| 0:42–0:48 | **The pathway**: invitation → attended → result → follow-up; only the first step lights up | Finnish Cancer Registry |
| 0:48–0:55 | **What doesn't work and what does**: translated letters 46.5% vs 47.4%; a kit offered at the visit 56% vs ~13% by post | Norway trial; London study |

### 0:55–1:35 · Silta: each person through their door

Screen recordings composited onto the phone in their hands, with short insert shots of their
faces. Each step gets a 3–4 word caption.

> **VO (opening the section):** Silta is a bridge from "I noticed something" to the right door, in your own language.

| Time | Person | On the phone | Insert shot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:55–1:07 | **Yusuf** | At home he scans the QR and picks Arabic. He types "stomach pain, blood when I go, for weeks". **Gemini** matches it to a card and asks one follow-up question instead of guessing. Silta: *this is worth having checked*; his health station; a **Finnish call script** to read aloud. | He dials, reading from the phone. |
| 1:07–1:20 | **Leila** | The letter's QR. A **"what to expect" card, read aloud (ElevenLabs)**: who takes the sample, how long it takes, what you can ask for. **How to change the time.** Her preferences are saved. The option to ask for a **home self-sampling kit**. | Her shoulders drop; she circles a new date. |
| 1:20–1:35 | **Amina and Hodan** | One phone between them. Hodan taps **cards in Somali** and hears them aloud; she never has to say the word. Amina reads the call script. Caption: *Your mother has the right to a free professional interpreter.* Then the visit: the **nurse reads the one-screen Finnish summary**, with the preferences and the kit prompt. | Amina waiting outside the exam room. |

### 1:35–1:50 · What a county does

Clean caption cards; the VO reads them.

> **VO:** For a county, this is three things.
> 1. Put Silta where people already are: the invitation letter, pharmacies, the health-station web page.
> 2. At the visit: the Finnish summary, preferences known in advance, and a prompt to offer a home kit.
> 3. Measure it with the registry table you already get, against sub-goal 1.3.

One impact line from Mea's slider: *Closing a quarter of the gap ≈ X more screenings a year.*

### 1:50–2:00 · Close

The triptych again, now resolved: Yusuf on the phone, Leila walking into the health station, Hodan
and Amina with the interpreter. Then the end card:

> **Silta — a bridge to the right door.**
> Not a diagnosis · no identity · sources and limits at `/dashboard`
> Gemini · ElevenLabs · Aiven · Timo · Mea · Milla

three set-ups) → venue (exam room, team) → Sunday morning outdoors (pharmacy, mail slot, health
station).

## Preparation

### Props

- **Silta QR stand** for the pharmacy counter and a **QR sticker** for the letter. Silta's own design,
  pointing at the consumer app.
- **A generic screening letter**, printed, with a QR. Finnish text drafted from the real wording
  structure, but no county or HUS logo, no letterhead, no real names: it must read as "a screening
  invitation", not as a copy of an official one.
- Two or three stomach and bowel medicine boxes (bought), a calendar, tea things, a tablet for the nurse.

### Cast

- Yusuf: an Arabic-speaking man about 50+.
- Leila: a woman about 35–45, comfortable with the topic; can be partly anonymous.
- Amina and Hodan: ideally a real Somali-speaking daughter and mother, or friends who can play them.
  Fallback: Amina's face and Hodan's hands and voice.
- Nurse and interpreter: teammates or friends.
- Everyone gives written consent; the scenes are presented as illustrative, never as real patients.
  The cast write their own lines in their language; Gemini can draft, they rewrite.

### Screen recordings (check with Mea that each works in the demo build)

- QR → language picker (Arabic) → free text → Gemini card match + follow-up question → "worth checking" → right door → call script.
- Letter QR → "what to expect" card with audio → changing the time → preferences → self-sampling kit option.
- Somali cards with audio → right door → call script → interpreter-right card → visit card → clinician summary (Finnish) with preferences and kit prompt.
- County view (for the 1:35 beat, optional).

### Graphics

- Four data cards and the pathway animation, built from Mea's CSVs with the site palette.
- The county-actions card, the end card, name captions.
- Burned-in English captions; Somali and Arabic on screen checked by a native speaker.

### Audio

- VO: one calm voice (Timo or Milla), ≈ 230 words, recorded in a quiet room.
- In-app audio: the ElevenLabs clips from the app itself, heard in the scenes.
- Music: a quiet instrumental bed (generated or royalty-free), low under the voice.

## Check before filming

- [ ] **Every number** on the data cards against Mea's sources; the impact-slider figure.
- [ ] **Who takes the cervical screening sample** in Finland and what Silta's "what to expect" card says
      about it. Leila's fear of a male examiner is answered by what the card truthfully says, not by a
      promise in the video.
- [ ] **How to change a screening time** is in the app's content for the demo county.
- [ ] **Preferences, the kit prompt, the interpreter card and the clinician summary** work in the demo build.
- [ ] **Pharmacy QR** is shown as a proposal for counties ("where a county could put Silta"), not as
      something pharmacies already do.
- [ ] Permission from the pharmacy, if we film inside one.

## Why this balance works

| Criterion | Where it's covered |
| --- | --- |
| Actionability 30% | 1:35–1:50: three county actions, including where the QR goes (letters, pharmacies, web). |
| Insight 25% | 0:42–0:55: the gap opens at the first step; translation alone doesn't work, acting at the visit does. |
| Data quality 20% | A source line on every card; the end card with limits and `/dashboard`. |
| Execution 15% | 0:55–1:35: three real flows in three languages. |
| Clarity 10% | One question in the bridge, one key message, three headline numbers. |
| Audience | Three recognisable people in the first 24 seconds, resolved at the end. |
| Tech prizes | Gemini (the card match), ElevenLabs (audio in Arabic and Somali), Aiven on the end card (and the county view). |
