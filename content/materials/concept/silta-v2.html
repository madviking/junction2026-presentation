Silta

silta (Finnish) = bridge

Earlier cancer detection for people in Finland who don't speak Finnish.

Women born in Africa attend cervical screening at 46%, against 73% for women born in Finland. Silta takes a person from a letter she cannot read, or a symptom she cannot explain, to a doctor who understands her. It gives the county the one thing translated letters have not delivered: actions that are proven to raise participation, and a way to see whether they work.

So the design rule is: every piece of information in Silta ends in an action. A booking, a kit, a call script, a card the doctor can read.

One person, one crossing

Hodan, 54, speaks Somali and lives in Helsinki

The same steps work for any language and any symptom. We tell it through one person because that is how the demo runs.

A QR code on the screening letter

The letter is in Finnish. The QR opens Silta in Somali, with no install and no sign-up.

  • Also on waiting-room posters and in community groups

What this letter means, and what to do

Plain answers about screening, symptoms, rights and costs, read aloud on request.

  • Every answer names its Finnish public source
  • Says clearly that it is an AI and does not diagnose
AI: answers grounded in a vetted set of pages; clinical terms from the phrase set
3 · PREPARE

She has also noticed a lump

She taps picture cards: where, since when, changing or not, what worries her.

  • Body map and symptom checklist
  • Preferences: female clinician, interpreter language
  • Emergency signs are always on screen
AI: voice input in languages where it is accurate; read-back to confirm
4 · NAVIGATE

The right door in Helsinki

Where to book, what it costs, and a Finnish call script to read aloud or show.

  • Two or three plain questions pick the path (see safety below)
  • How to ask for a free professional interpreter
AI: fills the Finnish script from fixed templates. Routing is rules, not the model.
5 · THE VISIT

Cards in both directions

The doctor reads a one-screen Finnish summary on Hodan's phone or a printout, then answers with cards.

  • Clinician taps Finnish cards; Hodan sees and hears Somali
  • Overdue for cervical screening: offer a self-sampling kit now
  • Works beside an interpreter, never instead of one
AI: none live. Every card is fixed, checked and recorded.
6 · AFTER

The plan, in Somali, from the doctor's own taps

"Referral sent", "lab test", "we call within 2 weeks": the cards the doctor chose become Hodan's next-steps list.

  • Typical and legal waiting times
  • When and whom to call if nothing arrives
AI: none needed. No guessing from what the patient remembers.

  • Gap to the 80% target and to parity

AI: drafts outreach material in the top languages, with back-translation for human review
For the county

What a wellbeing services county can do on Monday

This is the side the AstraZeneca brief scores highest. Silta does not hand the county a dashboard and leave. It proposes actions that have trial evidence behind them.

See the gap

  • Screening participation against the 80% target, by county
  • Share of residents by language, and the top languages
  • Helsinki has the lowest breast screening participation in Finland, 74.3%, and about 16,000 Somali speakers
  • Counts from Silta itself: scans, steps reached, where people stop

Act with a bundle

  • Kit at the visit: offer the self-sampling kit when an overdue person is already in the room
  • A call in their language: script and navigator, not a second letter
  • A community partner with a ready toolkit
  • Paper and SMS stay. Nothing becomes digital-only.

Measure against the strategy

  • Sub-goal 1.3: participation of people with another mother tongue reaches the level of Finnish and Swedish speakers
  • Goal 2: 80% in every programme, every county
  • Goal 3: treatment starts within target times; cancer mortality down 10%
  • Roadmap action 1.3.1 already funds multilingual screening communication: €400k, 2026–2028
Pilot measure, to be agreed with the county

One language group, one area, one programme: cervical screening among Somali-speaking women in one Helsinki district, measured against the 46% baseline for women born in Africa. The team still needs to set the target figure and the time frame.

Our edge

One checked phrase set does four jobs

Picture and phrase cards are not new. What is new is using one fixed, native-speaker-checked set for the whole path, so the same meaning travels from the patient to the doctor, back again, and into the county's numbers.

Hodan sees and hearsKuus ayaan ka dareemay naaskaExample wording. A native speaker checks and records every card.
The doctor readsTunsin kyhmyn rinnassaI felt a lump in my breast
1. PrepareThe patient taps cards; the answers form the visit summary.
2. Be understoodThe same cards are pointed at in the room when words run out.
3. Understand the doctorThe clinician's cards carry the plan back in the patient's language.
4. Inform the countyEach card is a structured field, so anonymous counts need no text analysis.

Why fixed phrases

  • The person picks the meaning, so nothing is misheard or mistranslated.
  • Works with low literacy: a picture and a human voice.
  • Works offline and on old phones once the cards are cached.
  • A new language costs one review and one recording session, with no dependence on any speech vendor.

Where voice AI comes in

  • Russian, Ukrainian, Estonian, Turkish and Vietnamese have excellent speech recognition in ElevenLabs' own tiers: full voice flow.
  • Somali and Kurdish are rated moderate, which means 25–50% of words wrong: cards first.
  • These are also the languages with the largest screening gap, so the cards serve the people who need Silta most.

Safety, privacy, regulation

Three lines Silta does not cross

No judgement on a person's symptoms

  • No risk score and no urgency rating. That would make Silta a medical device.
  • One fixed list of emergency signs, always visible, pointing to 112.
  • Routing comes from written rules: county plus situation. The model never picks the door.
  • The clinician sees one factual prompt: screening overdue under the national programme. No "tests to consider".

Health answers stay on the phone

  • Default: nothing about the person is stored on a server. The summary is shown on the phone or printed.
  • Optional: a short-lived share code if the clinician wants it on their own screen. It expires the same day.
  • No name, identity code, phone number or email, ever.
  • Voice audio is transcribed and discarded. Free text warns against typing names.
  • Quick-exit button and no visible history, for phones that others check.

No questions that cost trust

  • The path depends on residence status, but Silta never asks about status directly.
  • It asks: "Do you have a Kela card?", "Do you work in Finland?", "Which municipality do you live in?"
  • Those answers stay on the phone and the screen says so.
  • The county receives counts only: language, area, step reached.
How people find it

Three channels the county already owns or trusts

The screening letter

A QR code on invitations and reminders. It reaches exactly the people who are due, at the moment they are deciding, and every scan is measurable.

The health system's own doors

County websites, the 116117 waiting message, posters in health centres, maternity clinics, pharmacies and reception centres.

Community partners

Associations, mosques and churches, health ambassadors, and a shareable card per topic for the messaging groups people already use.

Search pages per language, citation by AI assistants and free search ads are kept as later work in v1. They do not need to be in the pitch.

The two-minute demo

One county (Helsinki), one persona, one screening path (cervical self-sampling), one symptom (breast lump), and two languages: Somali on cards and one voice language.

  1. 15 sThe gap: 46% against 73%. A translated letter does not fix it.
  2. 20 sHodan scans the QR on her letter. Silta opens in Somali and explains it aloud.
  3. 25 sShe has also felt a lump. She taps cards, gets the Helsinki booking path and a Finnish call script.
  4. 25 sAt the health centre the doctor reads the Finnish summary and answers with cards. Hodan hears the plan in Somali.
  5. 15 sSame visit: screening overdue, kit offered on the spot. 56% take it this way.
  6. 20 sCounty screen: Helsinki's gap, the scans and drop-offs, one recommended bundle with its measure.

Research backlog

Service paths

  • Helsinki first: health centre booking, phone numbers, opening hours, Maisa, Omaolo, 116117, emergency clinics. Source: the national service registry (PTV, open API) and county sites.
  • Paths by situation: home municipality, occupational health, students, EU citizens, temporary protection, asylum seekers, undocumented people.
  • Screening paths: invitation, booking, self-sampling pilots, follow-up after a positive result.

Waiting times

  • Primary care: share of non-urgent visits waiting over 7 days, by county (THL Sotkanet 6411).
  • Legal limits for primary care, specialist assessment and treatment; the 6-week recommendation for cancer.
  • Screening follow-up delays by county (Cancer Registry).

Bottlenecks

  • Getting a first appointment; repeat visits before diagnosis.
  • Interpreter availability and booking lead times.
  • Results and letters in Finnish only; OmaKanta in three languages.
  • Client fees and no-show fees.
What changed from v1
  • Opening: leads with cancer screening and one person's story, with the evidence at the top and a source under each figure.
  • County: moved from the gap list into the journey as step 7, with its own section on actions and measures.
  • Entry point: the QR on the screening letter is step 1. The long discoverability section became three channels.
  • Edge: restated from "cards where voice is weak" to "one checked phrase set does four jobs".
  • Cards go both ways: the clinician answers with cards, and those taps become the after-visit plan. This replaces the AI explaining what the patient remembers.
  • Clinician prompts: "tests to consider" removed. Only the overdue-screening prompt and the kit offer remain.
  • Privacy: on the phone by default, with an optional same-day share code, in place of server storage against a code for 90 days. This is a proposal; the team decided on the individual code on 13 Nov.
  • Audiences: narrowed to language minorities, clinicians and counties. The other groups from v1 still benefit but are not in the pitch.
  • New: comparison with existing services, the demo script, and the list of real and mocked parts.
  • Moved down: the voice coverage table, open items and research backlog are now in this working area.
