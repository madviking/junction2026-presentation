Silta

silta (Finnish) = bridge

From “something feels wrong” to the right care in Finland, in your own language.

Built first for people who don't speak Finnish well, and useful for anyone who finds the health system hard to navigate. Silta helps people find services, prepare for a visit, understand the path through care, and be understood by the doctor. It helps clinicians get a clearer, faster visit, and gives counties a view of where language minorities fall out of the pathway.

Who it serves

Language minorities first, everyone welcome

The six goals, as one journey

Across the bridge

Each step lists what the person gets and where AI helps. The sixth step is the clinician's side of the same visit.

1 · FIND

Service information in my language

Plain-language answers about symptoms, screening, services and rights.

  • Search or ask in your language
  • Every answer cites a Finnish public source
  • Read aloud for low literacy
AI: grounded answers over a vetted corpus; translation with a checked phrase library
2 · PREPARE

Get ready for the visit

Describe what's happening by speaking, tapping pictures or writing.

  • Body map and symptom checklist (not a diagnosis)
  • Since when, getting worse, other signs
  • Preferences: female clinician, interpreter, comfort with a cervical sample
AI: speech-to-text, structuring free text into the checklist, read-back to confirm
3 · NAVIGATE

Who to contact, in my region

The right door for your county and situation, and what to expect.

  • Health centre, occupational health, 116117, 112
  • Finnish call script to read aloud or show
  • How to ask for a free interpreter; costs and fees
AI: picks the path from county + situation; generates the Finnish script
4 · AT THE VISIT

Be understood

Flash cards to point at when words fail, alongside an interpreter.

  • Cards for symptoms, pain scale, duration, body parts
  • Each card shows Finnish and your language
  • Show the prepared visit card by QR
AI: none needed live; cards are pre-translated and checked
5 · AFTER

Know what happens next

What the doctor said, in your language, and when to expect what.

  • Referral, tests, results in OmaKanta
  • Typical and legal waiting times
  • When and how to chase if nothing arrives
AI: plain-language explanation of what you type or say the doctor told you
6 · FOR THE CLINICIAN

A prepared, structured visit

A one-screen Finnish summary the patient brings.

  • Symptoms already marked, in Finnish
  • Preferences known in advance
  • Prompts: tests to consider; overdue screening → offer a self-sampling kit
AI: Finnish summary with back-translation; prompts from rules, not the model
Privacy model

An individual code instead of an identity

Silta never asks for a name, personal identity code, phone number or email. Health information is stored only against a random code that the person holds.

SLT · 7K3Q · 9PXM
1The app creates a random code on the phone. No sign-up.
2The person prepares the visit; answers are saved against the code, encrypted.
3At the visit they show the code or a QR; the clinician opens a read-only Finnish summary.
4The data deletes itself after a set time (for example 90 days), or at once on request.

What still has to be true

  • Pseudonymous health data can still be personal data under GDPR if it can be linked to a person, so a data protection impact assessment and a clear data controller (the county, in a pilot) are needed.
  • Voice audio goes to ElevenLabs for transcription: process and discard it, never store recordings, and check ElevenLabs' data-processing and retention terms.
  • Losing the code means losing the data, by design. Offer a printable card with the code.
  • Analytics are anonymous counts only (language, county, step reached).
  • A quick-exit button and no history on shared phones, for people whose phone is checked by others.

Our edge

Cards first where voice AI is weak

For Somali and Kurdish, Silta does not try to make poor speech recognition and synthetic voices work. It uses a simplified flash-card flow: pictures, a body map and short phrases, each card checked by a native speaker and paired with audio recorded by a human voice. The person taps; the card shows Finnish to the clinician and their own language to them.

The same cards power the visit-preparation checklist and the in-visit communication, so one checked phrase set does both jobs. These are also the languages where the screening gap is largest: women born in Africa attend cervical screening at 46%, and Somali-background women at 19% in an earlier study.

Why this is stronger

  • No transcription errors to catch: the person picks the meaning, so nothing is misheard.
  • No machine-translation risk in clinical terms: every phrase is fixed and reviewed.
  • Works for low literacy: pictures plus a human voice reading the card.
  • Works offline and on old phones once the cards are cached.
  • Extends to Albanian, Tigrinya, Dari and others with no ElevenLabs support at all: record the phrases once, reuse them everywhere.
  • Voice stays available for languages where it is excellent (Russian, Ukrainian, Estonian, Turkish, Vietnamese).
Discoverability

How people find Silta when they need it

The best tool is useless if nobody finds it at the moment of worry. Search matters, but the strongest channels are places people already trust.

Search, in the user's language

  • One page per question, language and region: “lump in breast what to do Helsinki” written in Somali, Arabic, Russian, with real native wording, not translated keywords.
  • Answer the exact questions people type: how to see a doctor, is an interpreter free, what does a referral mean.
  • Technical basics: hreflang per language, structured data (MedicalWebPage, FAQPage, HowTo), fast pages that work on old phones.
  • Free search ads: if Silta is run by a non-profit, Google's Ad Grants programme gives free search advertising.

AI assistants and voice search

  • Be the source AI cites: clear, structured, openly licensed pages with sources, so ChatGPT, Gemini and others point people to Silta.
  • A machine-readable summary (llms.txt) and a public content API.
  • Voice-search phrasing: people with low literacy ask by voice, in full sentences.

Official touchpoints

  • A QR code in screening invitation letters and reminders, leading to Silta in the person's language.
  • Links from county websites (Länsi-Uusimaa already translates its site into 11 languages), Infopankki, Omaolo and the 116117 waiting message.
  • Posters in waiting rooms, maternity and child clinics, pharmacies, reception centres.
  • Welcome packs: Kela, Migri, integration training and Finnish language courses.

Communities and word of mouth

  • Community organisations, mosques, churches and cultural associations; trained health ambassadors.
  • Messaging groups people already use (WhatsApp, Telegram, Viber): a shareable card per topic.
  • Media in their languages: Yle's news in Arabic, Somali, Russian and Ukrainian, community radio, short videos.
  • “Help someone else” mode, so the family member who usually translates brings Silta along.

Research backlog

What we need to find out

Service paths

  • For Helsinki and Länsi-Uusimaa first: health centre booking, phone numbers, opening hours, Maisa, Omaolo, 116117, emergency clinics. Source: the national service registry (PTV, open API) and county sites.
  • Separate paths by situation: resident with a home municipality, occupational health, students, EU citizens, Ukrainians under temporary protection and asylum seekers (reception centre health care), undocumented people (e.g. Global Clinic).
  • Screening paths: invitation, booking, self-sampling pilots (5 counties in 2026), follow-up after a positive result.

Waiting times

  • Primary care: share of non-urgent visits waiting over 7 days by county (THL Sotkanet 6411, 2025: 32.7–85.4%).
  • Legal limits: primary care (relaxed in 2025), specialist assessment within 3 weeks, treatment within 6 months; ministry recommendation 6 weeks from referral to treatment for cancer.
  • Screening follow-up delays by county (Cancer Registry, colonoscopy and colposcopy delay charts).

Bottlenecks

  • Getting a primary-care appointment; nurse first contact; repeat visits before diagnosis (a third needed 3 or more).
  • Interpreter availability and booking lead times; use of family members.
  • Results and letters in Finnish only; OmaKanta in Finnish, Swedish and English.
  • Cost barriers: client fees and no-show fees.
