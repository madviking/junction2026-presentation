The single story that the video, the live pitch, the deck and the partner pages all branch from. Screening leads; the symptom route supports it. Every number below has a source in the team brain.

The spine, in one breath

People in Finland whose native language is not Finnish or Swedish attend cancer screening about 20 points less often, in all three programmes, and the gap is growing. It opens at the very first step, getting through the door. Silta is a working, multilingual bridge across that step: it explains the letter, prepares the visit and gets the self-sampling kit offered in the room. It is safe to use today without anything from the wellbeing services county, and every step a county adds makes it worth more.

Beat III · Insight

Why the door stays shut

The gap is at the first step. Breast and cervical follow-up after an abnormal result is equal. So the fix belongs at the invitation and the visit, and it has to work for someone who cannot read the letter, would not say the words out loud, and does not know which number to call.

Language

Omaolo, Maisa, Lunna and 116117 work in Finnish, Swedish and partly English, mostly behind bank ID. Non-native speakers are 4.8% of residents but 3.0% of digital-clinic users.

Silta: 14 languages, read aloud, no login.

An intimate exam by a stranger, symptoms you would not name to your own daughter. A gynaecological check-up in the past five years is the strongest predictor of cervical screening among immigrant women. Faith-based peer education raised mammography in a mosque pilot (indicative, n = 58).

Silta: tap instead of say; preferences sent ahead; a kit used at home.

Beat IV · Product

What Silta does, screening first

One app, two ways in. The screening letter leads the story. "I noticed something" uses the same steps and appears second.

1 · Letter

Understand it

QR code on the invitation, or a photo of any letter, explained in your language.

2 · Learn

See what happens

Five-part picture guide: why, the visit, your rights, after, questions. Read aloud.

3 · Prepare

Say it without words

Picture cards and body map. Preferences: interpreter language, female clinician.

4 · Visit

Be understood

Visit code → one-screen Finnish summary; prompt to offer a self-sampling kit.

5 · After

Know what's next

What happens and when; when to call if nothing arrives.

The symptom route, second

"I noticed something" goes through the same steps: general guidance for that kind of change, the right door for your entitlement in your area, a Finnish call script with its meaning and audio, and the legal clocks. It never rates the person's symptoms. We keep it because there is no Finnish data on the symptom route by language, so it supports the story and does not lead it.

Beat V · Trust

Compliance by design

Each rule is a design decision visible in the product, so a county's lawyer and a resident who found Silta by chance can both trust it.

EU MDR RULE 11 · MEDICAL DEVICES

Not a medical device. General guidance and who to contact. No diagnosis, no likelihood, no urgency rating of the person's own symptoms. The route lane comes from clinician-reviewed content, never from the model. 112 and 116117 on every screen.

GDPR ART. 9 · SPECIAL CATEGORIES

No personal data. No names, ID numbers, phone numbers or emails. A random visit code (SLT-XXXX-XXXX), stored hashed, expires after 90 days, deletable by the person. County data are anonymous aggregates; no individual targeting by language.

EU AI ACT · TRANSPARENCY

AI that asks before it acts. Gemini matches a person's own words to a card and asks back; one tap by the person before anything changes. Zero urgency or emergency decisions are left to a model.

PATIENT ACT §3, §5 · INTERPRETING

Supports the interpreter, never replaces one. The visit card states the language needed and the right to a free professional interpreter; clinical phrases come from a vetted phrase library.

CONTENT GOVERNANCE

Every fact has an owner and a date. Service-path facts publish only with a source and a verified-on date; texts and audio go through approval; every admin change is audited and can be rolled back.

DATA RESIDENCY

Kept in the EU. Database and app in the same Google Cloud EU region on Aiven.

Q&A prep

Questions the jury will ask

Isn't this just translating letters?

No. Translated invitations alone were tested in Norway and did nothing (46.5% vs 47.4%). Silta's main action is at the visit: a prepared patient and a kit offered in the room, which took 56% vs ~13% by post in London.

Is it a medical device?

No, by design under EU MDR Rule 11: general information and who to contact, no rating of a person's own symptoms, route lanes set by clinician-reviewed content, emergency numbers always on screen.

What do you store about people?

Nothing that identifies them. A random visit code, stored hashed, that expires after 90 days and can be deleted. The county sees anonymous counts only.

Machine translation in Somali is risky. How do you handle it?

Clinical phrases come from a vetted phrase library; Somali uses picture cards and human-recorded audio instead of weak speech AI; the visit card always asks for a professional interpreter. Native-speaker and clinical review is part of the pilot.

Why would a county adopt it?

It doesn't have to adopt anything to start. The first rung is a QR code on a letter it already sends; the kit-in-the-room rung is the cheapest channel per extra screen (€45 vs €360). Helsinki receives about €121M a year for foreign-language residents, unearmarked, and roadmap action 1.3.1 funds exactly this work.

How will you know it works?

With numbers the county already gets: the registry's attendance by native language, colonoscopy rate and delay after a positive test. Silta adds anonymous counts of where people stop before the first contact.

Is the business only Finland?

The same screening gap exists for language minorities across Nordic and EU programmes. Silta's model is structured service-path data per area plus vetted content per language; a new county is a data file and a review, not a rebuild.

Data transparency

What we don't claim

Saying these out loud scores on the 20% data-quality criterion and protects us in Q&A.

Language is not proven to cause the gap.

It survives controls for breast and colorectal; for cervical, municipal variation tracks unemployment. Say "language minorities face a gap like the lowest-education group".

No lives-saved or net-savings figures.

Show cost per extra screen and break-even points only.

No symptom-route data by language exists.

That is why the symptom route plays second.

Religion is not in the registry.

Numbers are by native language, birth region or origin. Hodan's faith is part of her story, not a data claim.

County correlations are "where", not "why".

r = −0.85 across counties is driven by the capital region; never imply worse survival in multilingual counties.

Finnish mailed-kit trials were modest (+3–4 pp).

Pitch London's in-visit model, not "Finland proved it".

Cervical deaths are few (45 a year).

Frame cervical by precancers found and the 1.8× cancer rate in non-attenders.

Silta can't fix colonoscopy capacity.

Helsinki's 3.4-month wait is a system problem; name it separately.
