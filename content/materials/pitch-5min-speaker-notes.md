## 1. The talk in five lines

1. **Message:** People with another native language get the same screening letter, and 27% fewer of them come. The gap opens at the letter, before anyone in care sees them.
2. **What Silta does:** one printed code on that letter opens the screening explained in their language, read aloud, with the Finnish words to say at the desk.
3. **Ask (said twice, at 1:35 and at the close):** a pilot in Helsinki. The code on one cervical cancer screening invitation batch, Silta at the health stations, measured as attendance per 100 invited, by native language, against a batch without it.
4. **The wow moment:** a Somali screen, and then the phone says in Finnish *"Toivoisin, että näytteen ottaa nainen."* The judges are the other side, and they understand her.
5. **One voice.** Timo speaks. Mea drives the laptop. Milla hands out the letters and holds the backup phone.

## 4. Script (verbatim, about 145 words a minute)

`/` short pause · `//` full pause (one second) · **bold** = stress · [brackets] = stage directions

### A · 0:00–0:20 · `hodan` · 44 words
[Timo holds up a printed letter.]
> Meet Hodan. / She is fifty-four. She has lived in Helsinki for years. Her language is Somali.
> This is her second invitation to cervical cancer screening. / The same letter you have in your hands.
> She opens it. / Puts it in a drawer. / And doesn't go. //

### B · 0:20–0:45 · `gap` · 60 words
> [→ as Timo finishes A; wait for the heading to fly in] She is not alone.
> This is the Finnish Cancer Registry's own data. People whose native language is not Finnish or Swedish get the same letter, / and **27 percent** fewer of them come.
> Breast. Cervical. Colorectal. Every programme.
> That is about **sixteen and a half thousand** screenings missed every year. / And for breast and cervical screening, the gap has grown since 2020.

### C · 0:45–1:30 · `pathway` · 105 words
> So where does the gap open? [Tab. The map draws while you speak; don't wait in silence.]
> People reach cancer care three ways: / a screening letter, / a symptom, / or a test a doctor orders.
> Eleven steps. Only **three** of them are measured by native language, all three in screening.
> And the biggest gap is right at the start: / coming at all. After an abnormal cervical result, the difference is no longer significant. After a positive bowel test there is a second, smaller gap.
> [Turn from the screen to the judges.]
> What we don't claim: / we can't prove language causes this. We give no lives-saved figures. And Hodan is a composite.
> But the first step is where the gap opens, / and where it can be measured.

### D · 1:30–1:45 · `silta` · 30 words
> So we built Silta. [→] / Finnish for "bridge".
> Our proposal fits in one line: / **print one code on the screening letter Helsinki already sends, / and measure who comes.**

### E · 1:45–2:10 · `letter` · 52 words
> Here it is. [→, wait for the heading] One code, at the bottom of Hodan's letter.
> Why not just translate the letter? [→: the letter shrinks, the guide appears] Norway tested that in a randomised trial. **46.5 percent** came with a translated letter. **47.4** with the standard one. / Translation alone did nothing. //
> So Silta explains. / Let me show you. Live.

### F · 2:10–3:05 · LIVE DEMO · 84 words + about 10 s of audio
[Tab → T3]
> This is what the code opens. In Somali. / No app, no login, no name.

> // *"Toivoisin, että näytteen ottaa nainen."* //

### G · 3:05–3:25 · `visit` · 48 words

## 5. Numbers card: only these five are said aloud

| Say | Exact | Source (all on the deck) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **27% fewer** | relative drop, e.g. breast 60.8 ÷ 83.2 | Finnish Cancer Registry, screening by native language, 2024 (`gap`) |
| **about 16,500 missed a year** | ≈16,560: breast 5,556 + cervical 6,844 + colorectal 4,161 | Our calculation from Cancer Registry 2024 (`earlier` → For the county) |
| **3 of 11 steps** measured by language | all three in screening | Pathway slide → Limitations |
| **46.5% vs 47.4%** | translated vs standard invitation, n = 11,347 | Randomised trial, Norway (`letter` → For the county) |
| **€400,000** | for counties, HUS and NGOs, 2026–2028 | National Cancer Strategy roadmap 2026–2031, action 1.3.1 (`county` → In euros) |

**Never say:**
- **57 vs 78 next to 27%.** 78 − 57 = 21 points, not 27, and a judge who works it out will think the data is sloppy. 27% is the relative drop; percentage points are for Q&A.
- **"All three gaps are growing."** Colorectal is flat. Say "breast and cervical".
- **"Breast follow-up is equal."** Breast follow-up is reported only as a referral rate.
- **"Human-recorded Somali audio."** The live Somali text is machine-translated and its audio is marked unchecked (`tech/docs/proof/2026-11-14/voice.md`). Say "read aloud".
- **Lives saved, net savings, or that language causes the gap.**

## 7. Q&A (20-second answers)

Who answers: **Timo** takes 2, 3, 7 and the bonus. **Mea** takes 1 and 4 (and may open T2 and click a step; after that, use the on-screen arrow). **Milla** takes 5. Anyone can take 6.

1. **"Is it really language, not education or income?"** We don't claim it causes the gap. Across about 290 municipalities, language still predicts lower breast and colorectal attendance after controlling for education, age and unemployment. For cervical, it tracks unemployment. Africa-born women attend 26% less even after adjusting for age, education and residence. Language is where the register can see the gap, and where people can be reached. *(`safe` → What we don't claim; `gap` → For Hodan)*
2. **"Counties already translate letters. Why would this work?"** A translated letter alone did nothing in Norway: 46.5% against 47.4%, with 11,347 women. Silta adds what a letter can't: what happens in the room, her rights, how to rebook, and the Finnish words to say. Whether that raises attendance is exactly what the pilot measures. *(`letter` → For the county)*
3. **"What does the county have to do, and what does it cost?"** Print one block on a letter it already sends, and put up posters. No integration and no personal data, so there is no IT project. We haven't priced a pilot, and we won't guess. The money exists: €400k in roadmap action 1.3.1, and about €121M a year reaches Helsinki through the foreign-language funding criterion, none of it earmarked. *(`county` → In euros)*
4. **"Goal 3 is treatment on time and survival. Isn't this screening?"** Goal 3 is measured by treatment times, mortality and survival, and none of them is split by native language. Screening attendance is upstream of all three, and it is the strategy's own sub-goal 1.3. There is a second measured gap after a positive bowel test: 68% have the colonoscopy, against 77% (2022–24). That is the move from primary to specialist care, and Silta explains it too. *(`pathway` → follow-up step)*
5. **"Who checked the Somali?"** Honestly: it was machine-translated this weekend, and our admin marks it unchecked. A native-speaker and clinical review, with a community organisation, is the pilot's first task. The facts come from HUS and City of Helsinki pages, each with a source and a checked date. Every change is logged and can be rolled back.
6. **"Is it a medical device? GDPR? AI?"** It never diagnoses or rates urgency (EU MDR Rule 11), and a button to 112 is on every screen. It stores no names, IDs, phone numbers or emails; a visit code is stored hashed and expires after 90 days. AI only suggests a card and the person confirms it; the route comes from reviewed content, never from the model. In a pilot, the county becomes the data controller. *(`safe`)*
7. **"Helsinki isn't a wellbeing services county."** Right. Helsinki organises its own services, and it is where the numbers are: 147,482 foreign-language residents (21.2%), with Somali its second-largest foreign language. Länsi-Uusimaa is a wellbeing services county and is one of our five mapped counties, so it works the same. *(`gap` → For the county)*

**Bonus, "What do you need from us?"** An introduction to the screening lead in Helsinki or HUS, and a medical-affairs reviewer for the content. We bring the working product.
