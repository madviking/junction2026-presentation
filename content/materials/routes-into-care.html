Where people fall out of cancer care

People reach cancer care through a screening letter, a symptom they notice, or a test a doctor orders. On each route, people whose native language is not Finnish, Swedish or Sámi meet gaps that Silta is built for. Only the screening route is measured by language, and there the gap is large.

Limitations

  • Only 3 of the 11 steps are recorded by native language, all of them in screening. Beyond screening we rely on studies grouped by origin or care sector, and some steps have no data at all.
  • “Other native language” pools many languages and includes people whose language is unknown. We use native language as a proxy for needing language support, which it only approximates.
  • The symptom-route figures come from one lymphoma study (160 patients) and a 2010–12 survey grouped by origin. They show which way the gap runs, not how large it is for the people Silta serves.
  • We show a difference only when its 95% confidence interval excludes zero. This left out colposcopy after an abnormal cervical result (+2.3 pp, not significant), waiting times (county medians, not tested by language) and digital-clinic contact rates (descriptive only).
