# Silta — 5-minute AstraZeneca judging talk + live demo (draft: Fable)

Sunday 15 Nov, 14:00. Judges: Hanna Rusila, Laura Aaltonen (Marketing), Joanna Tuormaa (Business Excellence). Non-technical, Finnish-speaking decision-makers.

Checked against the live site at 00:20–00:40 on 15 Nov: deck, `/try`, the letter, the Somali guide and both audio clips (Somali read-aloud and the Finnish desk phrase) all loaded and played.

---

## 1. Strategy (10 lines)

1. **One sentence:** "People with another native language get the same screening letter and 27% fewer come; one printed QR code on that letter takes them from the letter to a visit both sides understand."
2. **The ask:** a Helsinki pilot. The code on cervical screening invitations, Silta at the health stations, measured with the Cancer Registry's own attendance-by-native-language table.
3. **Say the ask twice:** once at 1:35 (before the demo, so the "message + action in under 2 minutes" criterion is met on the clock) and once at the close.
4. **Structure follows the rubric weights.** Insight (gap + where on the pathway) 0:20–1:35. Execution (live demo) 1:50–3:05. Actionability (what a county does, in three steps, and how it is measured) 3:25–4:45, the last thing they hear. Data transparency is spoken in every segment ("the registry's own data") and gets its own "what we don't claim" moment.
5. **Demo sits in the middle, in place of the four product slides** (`letter`, `noticed`, `door`, `visit`). The problem has just been located at the letter, so the demo starts at the letter. Those slides become the fallback.
6. **Demo = the letter route only.** It is the route with data, it needs no typing and no AI call, every page is a plain link, and it runs in about 70 seconds. The symptom route gets one slide (20 s).
7. **The wow moment is audio, and it is in Finnish:** Hodan's Somali screen speaks "Toivoisin, että näytteen ottaa nainen." The judges understand it instantly. That is the bridge.
8. **One speaker (Timo), one driver (Mea)** at the laptop. Milla hands each judge a printed letter before we start; its QR code is the leave-behind and the proof it is live.
9. **Six browser tabs in running order; one key (Ctrl+Tab) means "next scene".** No slide skipping on screen, no hunting.
10. **Nothing is said that is not on a slide or drill-in with a source.** No lives saved, no "language causes it", no savings.

## 3. Script (spoken, verbatim)

Word counts exclude stage directions.

### Segment 1 — `hodan` — 0:00–0:20 — 39 words
[Timo holds a printed screening letter.]

> This is Hodan. She is fifty-four. She has lived in Helsinki for years. Her language is Somali.
> [pause]
> This is her second invitation to cervical screening. It is in Finnish. She opens it, puts it away,
> [letter into jacket pocket]
> and she doesn't go.

### Segment 2 — `gap` — 0:20–0:50 — 62 words

> Hodan is not alone. [→]
> This is the Finnish Cancer Registry's own data, from 2024. People whose native language is not Finnish or Swedish get the same letter. And **27 percent fewer** come.
> Breast screening. Cervical. Colorectal. Every programme.
> That is **one in nine** people in Finland. And for breast and cervical screening, the gap is growing.
> [pause]
> Same letter. A quarter fewer people.

### Segment 3 — `earlier` — 0:50–1:00 — 27 words

> Why does it matter? [→] Screening finds cancer early. Cervical screening finds it before it is cancer at all. Early is easier to treat, and it costs less.

### Segment 4 — `routes` → `pathway` — 1:00–1:35 — 79 words

> So where on the care pathway does this gap open? [→]
> People reach cancer care three ways: [→] a screening letter, a symptom, or a test a doctor orders.
> [the map is drawing; point at the top line]
> The solid black line is the only part anyone measures by language. And the gap opens at the very first step: **57 percent** attend, against **78**.
> After that the lines go grey. Referral, diagnosis, treatment start: nobody counts those by language.
> So the place to act is the first step. The letter.

### Segment 5 — `silta` — 1:35–1:50 — 39 words

> So we built Silta. [→] Finnish for bridge.
> Our proposal fits in one line: **print one QR code on the screening letter Helsinki already sends.**
> Let me show you what Hodan gets. This is the live product, not a recording.

### Segment 6 — DEMO — 1:50–3:05 — 99 words + about 8 s of audio

> [Next tab: letter] Same letter. Same Finnish. One new thing, at the bottom: a code.
>
> She scans it. [Next tab: Somali guide] No app to install. No login. No name. And it is in Somali.
> She does not have to read it. She can listen. [click speaker — let the Somali voice run 4 seconds — click to stop]
> Five short parts with pictures: why it matters, what happens at the visit, her rights, what comes after, common questions. [scroll]
>
> This part is her rights. [Next tab] She can ask for a woman to take the sample. She can ask for an interpreter.
> And here is the bridge. [click speaker on the first Finnish sentence]
> [AUDIO: "Toivoisin, että näytteen ottaa nainen."]
> [pause — look at the judges]
> Her phone says it for her. In Finnish. You understood that. So will the nurse.

### Segment 7 — `visit` — 3:05–3:25 — 46 words

> Second way in: a symptom. [Next tab] Olga has found a lump. She answers in Russian, by tapping pictures.
> Silta shows her the right service in her county. And at the visit, the doctor reads this: one screen, in Finnish.
> Silta never rates a symptom and never diagnoses.

### Segment 8 — `county` — 3:25–4:05 — 92 words

> So what does a county have to do? [→] Almost nothing. That is the point.
> Step zero: [click "How a county joins"] Silta works today, on its own.
> Step one: print the QR code on the invitation letters. One printed block. No IT project. No integration. No personal data.
> Step two: posters and cards at the health stations.
> And the county measures it [Esc] with a table it already gets: the registry's attendance by native language. If the gap closes, it shows there.
> The national cancer strategy already funds this kind of work: **400,000 euros** for multilingual screening communication.

### Segment 9 — `safe` — 4:05–4:22 — 42 words

> And what we don't claim. [→, click "What we don't claim"]
> We don't claim language is proven to cause the gap. We don't claim lives saved. Hodan and Olga are composites.

### Segment 10 — `close` — 4:22–4:45 — 49 words

> [takes the letter back out of the pocket]
> Hodan's next letter carries a code. This time, she knows what it says.
> [pause] Thank you.

- **`letter` and `noticed` open with a 2-second animation** of the small heading. That is why the plan never passes through them, except in the fallback.

Also: the live close slide reads "Silta works at the door, from today." The local file reads "…before the first appointment…". Decide which, deploy, and check before 13:00; the README says whatever is on `main` is what judges see.

## 5. Q&A — six likely questions, 20-second answers

**1. "How do you know this will bring more people to screening?"**
We don't yet, and we say so. What we know: a translated letter alone does not work. A Norwegian randomised trial with over 11,000 people got 46.5% against 47.4%. So Silta does more than translate: it explains the visit, her rights, how to rebook, and gives her the Finnish words. The pilot answers your question, with the registry's own table. *(Deck: `letter` → For the county.)*

**2. "What does the county actually have to do, and what does it cost?"**
Print one block on a letter it already sends, and put up posters. No integration, no personal data, so no IT project. We have not priced a pilot; we won't guess. The money exists: the national roadmap sets aside €400,000 for multilingual screening communication, and Helsinki gets about €121 million a year through the foreign-language funding criterion, none of it earmarked. *(Deck: `county` → How a county joins; In euros.)*

**3. "Is language really the reason they don't come?"**
We can't prove cause. Language, education and birthplace overlap. Across about 290 municipalities, language still predicts lower breast and colorectal attendance after controlling for education, age and unemployment; for cervical, it follows unemployment more. And Africa-born women are still 26% lower after adjusting for age, education and residence. Language is where the registry can see the gap. *(Deck: `safe` → What we don't claim; `gap` → For Hodan; `pathway` → Limitations.)*

**4. "The challenge is Goal 3, treatment on time. Isn't this screening?"**
Goal 3 is equal and effective care, measured by treatment times, mortality and survival. None of those is split by native language. The only place the gap is visible is the first step, so that is where we act and measure. Silta also covers the symptom route and what happens after a positive test, where colonoscopy follow-up is 68% against 77%. *(Deck: `pathway`; `close` source line: Goal 3 and sub-goal 1.3.)*

**5. "Is it safe? Health data, AI, medical advice?"**
It stores no names, IDs, phone numbers or emails; a visit code expires after 90 days. It never diagnoses and never rates urgency, so it stays outside medical-device rules; an urgent-help button to 112 is on every screen. The AI only matches a person's words to a card and asks "did you mean this?". The route comes from reviewed content, never from the model. *(Deck: `safe`.)*

**6. "Are the translations right? And what about people who can't read or don't scan codes?"**
Honest answer: the translations are machine-made this weekend. Before real use, each language needs a native speaker and a clinical check; Somali first. That is step one of the pilot. For people who don't read: every card is read aloud, in 14 languages, and there is a phone line you can just talk to. For reach beyond the letter: community posters and cards. *(Deck: `running` → ElevenLabs; `reach`; site: "Native-speaker and clinical review of every language" under Later.)*

**Bonus — "What do you need from us?"** An introduction to the screening lead in Helsinki or HUS, and a mentor from medical affairs for the content review. We bring the working product.

Who answers: Timo takes 1, 2, bonus. Mea takes 3, 4 (opens the `pathway` slide and clicks the step). Milla takes 6. Any of the three takes 5.

## 6. What I cut, and why

| Cut | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Slides `letter`, `noticed`, `door` | The live demo shows the same thing for real. They are the fallback |
| The full symptom journey, live | It needs 8–10 taps, creates a visit on the server, and one step calls an AI model. Too long and too risky for 70 seconds. It has no Finnish data by language, so the team's own rule is that it supports the story and does not lead it |
| `/try` phone-in-a-frame as the demo stage | It opens at the language picker, three or more taps from the guide, and the phone is small on a projector. Full-width tabs at 150% read better. `/try` is for the judges' own phones afterwards |
| Live county view | It shows test journeys, almost all in English. It invites "is this real data?" The deck drill-in says "local test data" honestly; open it only if asked |
| Admin, service-path data, 316 tests, tech partners (`running`) | Judges are marketing and business excellence. "It works" is proven by the demo |
| `reach` and `ways-in` (15 channels) | The ask needs one channel: the letter. Fifteen dilutes it. Good Q&A material |
| Euro figures (€15,100, €30,170, €1.95M, €121M) | Each needs a caveat to be honest, and four caveats don't fit. "Costs less" is said once without a number; the figures sit in drill-ins for Q&A |
| ≈16,560 missed screenings, ×2 by 2035 | Strong, but "27% fewer" and "57 against 78" already carry the gap. A third number blurs the first two. First reserve if a segment runs short: add after "the gap is growing" |
| 19% vs 67% (Somali-origin women) | One study, different years and method from the registry figures. Kept for Q&A 3 |
| `team` | Said in the handshake, not on the clock |
