# Silta: 5-minute AstraZeneca judging, run sheet and script (Opus draft)

Sunday 15 Nov, 14:00. Judges: AstraZeneca marketing and business excellence. Target length 4:40, hard stop 5:00.

---

## 1. Strategy (10 lines)

1. **Message:** *"People with another native language get the same screening letter, but 27% fewer of them come. The gap opens before the first appointment. Silta closes that step, and a county can start with one printed code on letters it already sends."*
2. **Ask (said once, at the end):** a pilot on one cervical screening invitation batch in Helsinki (or Vantaa–Kerava or Länsi-Uusimaa), measured as attendance per 100 invitees by native language against a batch without the code. To AstraZeneca: *"Help us open that door to one county."*
3. **Structure:** one person (Hodan), one number (27%), one place in the pathway (the first step), one live moment, one pilot. Each part maps to a rubric line. Insight (25%) is the pathway slide. Data (20%) is "only 3 of 11 steps are measured" plus "what we don't claim". Execution (15%) is the live guide. Actionability (30%) is the county steps 0–1–2 plus the funding line that already exists. Clarity (10%): message and ask are both said before 2:10.
4. **Where the demo goes:** right after the letter slide (2:10–3:15), because the demo **is** the pilot. A judge sees exactly what one printed QR strip would give a Somali-speaking woman, and then hears the phone speak **Finnish to the Finnish judges**. That is the wow moment: "both sides understand", shown in the room and not described.
5. **What the demo is not:** no symptom flow, no Gemini letter-photo, no live county view. Those take more taps, call AI, write data or show team test data. Low rubric gain, high risk.

## 5. Q&A prep (20-second answers, sourced)

**1. "Is 27% real? And isn't this about education or income, not language?"**
> 27% is relative: breast 60.8% vs 83.2%, so about 22 percentage points (Finnish Cancer Registry, 2024). The same holds in all three programmes. Across about 290 municipalities, language still predicts lower breast and colorectal attendance after controlling for education, age, urbanisation and unemployment. For cervical, it tracks unemployment. So we don't claim language causes it. We claim it is where people can be reached. *(Our analysis of Registry and Statistics Finland data; "What we don't claim" sheet on slide 12.)*

**2. "Counties already translate letters. Why would this work?"**
> Translation alone didn't work: in a Norwegian randomised trial of 11,347 women, 46.5% came with a letter in their language and 47.4% with the standard letter. Silta adds what a letter can't: what happens in the room, the right to ask for a woman or an interpreter, and Finnish sentences to show at the desk. Whether that raises attendance is exactly what the pilot measures.

**3. "What does the county actually have to do, and what does it cost?"**
> Step one is a printed QR strip on invitations it already sends. Step two is posters and visit-prep cards at health stations. No integration, no personal data. Funding exists: roadmap action 1.3.1 has €400k for 2026–2028 for multilingual screening communication, and Helsinki receives about €121M a year through the foreign-language funding criterion, none of it earmarked. We report cost per extra completed screen, not lives saved. *(National Cancer Strategy roadmap 2026–2031; Ministry of Finance criteria, our calculation.)*

**4. "How do you measure impact, and where is Goal 3?"**
> Measure: attendance per 100 invitees among other-language invitees, in a batch with the code versus a comparable batch without. The screening register already records it, and that is the strategy's own sub-goal 1.3. Goal 3 is measured by mortality, survival and treatment on time. Screening attendance is the step upstream of all three, and for this group it is where the gap opens. Silta also counts anonymously where people stop, by language. No register records that step today.

**5. "Who checked the Somali? Is the medical content right?"**
> Honest answer: the Somali text is machine-translated and marked "unchecked" in our admin, which tracks review status per language. Native-speaker and clinical review with a community organisation is the first task of the pilot. The facts come from HUS and City of Helsinki pages, each with a source and a checked date. Every change is logged and can be rolled back. For Somali, the symptom route uses picture cards instead of voice input, on purpose.

**6. "Is this a medical device? What about GDPR and AI?"**
> It is built to stay outside medical-device rules: no diagnosis and no urgency rating, and a 112 button on every screen (EU MDR Rule 11). No names, IDs, phones or emails. A visit code is stored hashed and expires after 90 days. AI only suggests a card, and the person confirms with one tap. The route comes from reviewed content, never from the model. In a pilot, the county becomes data controller and does its own impact assessment.

**If asked, short answers:**
- *Why Helsinki?* It has 147,482 foreign-language residents (21.2%), Somali is its second-largest foreign language, and with Vantaa–Kerava and Länsi-Uusimaa it holds 51% of Finland's foreign-language residents (Statistics Finland 2025). Länsi-Uusimaa is among our five mapped counties if AZ prefers a wellbeing services county proper.
- *Symptom route data?* None by language exists in Finland (National Cancer Strategy, section 2.5). That's why the symptom route supports the story and does not lead it.
- *Is it running?* Yes: 14 languages, service paths for 5 counties, 180+ source links. Scan the letter in your hand.
