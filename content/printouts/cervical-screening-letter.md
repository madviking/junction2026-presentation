# A screening letter that explains itself

**Every invitation carries one QR code. It opens a five-minute picture guide in the reader's own language.**

The letter is modelled on the real Helsinki / HUS invitation: a pre-booked time and place, the reservation key and PIN for changing it, the health form, and what to bring. It is stamped "example"; the person, address and codes are made up.

Why a guide and not just a translated letter: in a Norwegian randomised trial, invitations in the language of origin gave the same attendance as standard ones (46.5% vs 47.4%). The guide adds what a letter cannot: what happens in the room, the right to ask for a woman to take the sample or for an interpreter, Finnish sentences to show at the desk, and answers to the questions people hesitate to ask.

## What the guide covers

1. **Why it matters** — finds changes years before cancer; no symptoms; HPV is common and not a sign of unfaithfulness.
2. **What happens at the visit** — what to bring, the 10-minute visit, how the sample is taken, how it feels.
3. **Your rights** — ask for a woman to take the sample, an interpreter, someone you trust with you, a pause; it is free and your choice.
4. **After the visit** — result by post or OmaPosti in about two months; what a normal result and a colposcopy mean.
5. **Common questions** — pain, a positive HPV result, periods, pregnancy, never having had sex, female genital cutting, a removed uterus, changing the time, a lost letter, cost.
